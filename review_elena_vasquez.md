# Review: Certified Phase Atlases for Biological ODE Models

**Reviewer:** Prof. Elena Vasquez (Formal Methods & Program Verification, MIT)
**Score: 8/10**
**Expertise Weight: 0.8** (strong alignment with formal verification aspects)

---

The tiered certification architecture is the centerpiece of this proposal from a formal-methods perspective, and it is well-conceived. The three-tier approach — interval arithmetic recomputation (Tier 1), δ-complete SMT via dReal (Tier 2), and exact QF_NRA via Z3 (Tier 3) — correctly reflects the decidability landscape of nonlinear arithmetic over the reals. The automatic tier selection based on formula complexity is a practical contribution that other projects in verified numerics should emulate.

**Strengths.** The soundness theorem B1 for δ-complete SMT encoding is the strongest formal contribution. The explicit characterization of the gap between δ-soundness and exact soundness, combined with the certificate composition calculus (B2), constitutes a rigorous framework for proof management in hierarchical verification. The requirement for overlap or boundary certificates between adjacent regions handles a subtlety that naive composition approaches miss. The 59/59 equilibrium verification result across benchmark models is compelling evidence of practical feasibility — this is not a paper proof but a working verification pipeline. The decision to use SMT-LIB2 format for interoperable certificate output demonstrates good formal-methods engineering practice.

**Weaknesses.** The parameter-dependent Krawczyk theorem (A2) requires uniform contraction over the parameter fiber. Near bifurcation points, the Jacobian becomes nearly singular and the contraction constant degrades. The proposal acknowledges this but does not specify how the SMT encoding handles near-singularity — this is where the verification pipeline is most likely to produce false negatives (failing to certify a true regime). The adaptive refinement convergence proof (C1) assumes piecewise-smooth phase boundaries, which is a reasonable working hypothesis but excludes fractal boundaries that arise near chaotic regimes. More critically, the composition of two approximation layers — δ-perturbation from dReal and interval enclosures from Krawczyk — creates compounded error that must be tracked through the certificate. The proposal does not specify how these two error sources interact in the certificate format. Finally, the dimensional limitations (n ≤ 6 for equilibria, p ≤ 4 for systematic coverage) are honest but significantly constrain applicability to real systems-biology models, many of which have 10+ state variables.

**Verdict.** A well-designed verification pipeline with genuine formal-methods contributions. The tiered architecture with graceful degradation and the certificate composition calculus are publishable at CAV or TACAS. The main risk is scalability: if dReal cannot handle the target formulas (hence the critical importance of Milestone 0), the system falls back to Tier 1 only, which weakens the "machine-checkable" claim to "independently recomputed." The honest engineering methodology — including explicit go/no-go gates — inspires confidence.
